I am a cognitive psychologist and vision scientist at Goethe University, Frankfurt. My [research](/research) focusses on human scene categorization, our remarkable ability to make sense of our environment within fractions of a second. I use psychophysics, eyetracking and modelling to better understand which types of information and cognitive processes enable this feat. Besides visual perception, I am generally interested in the many ways our cognitive system "can be wrong", including phenomena such as illusions, memory distortions, and cognitive biases. As a lay magician, I study how these apparent "flaws" of the human mind can be used to deceive people in an entertaining way.

I obtained my PhD in psychology under the supervision of [Melissa Võ](https://www.mcn.uni-muenchen.de/members/regular/vo/index.html) in the [Scene Grammar Lab](https://www.scenegrammarlab.com/). As a PostDoc at Goethe University, Frankfurt, I represent the work unit for Allgemeine Psychologie I (i.e., cognitive psychology). 

I consider myself a [teacher](/teaching) at least as much as I consider myself a researcher. I am passionate about sharing my enthusiasm for cognitive psychology with psychology students at the Bachelor's and Master's level, and I value the exchange with undergraduate and graduate students I frequently supervise and collaborate with.

As an employee of a state-funded university, I am committed to democractic principles, and I am active in several academic commitees. In my work, I strive for open, transparent, and reproducible research in the spirit of open science.
